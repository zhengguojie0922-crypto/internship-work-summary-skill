# Achievement Analysis

## Ten Dimensions

Analyze each candidate major output in memory across these ten dimensions. Keep only supported statements, and mark gaps as unknown or needs user input:

1. **Problem and context**: identify the prior state, constraint, request, or defect.
2. **Technical scope**: identify components, layers, systems, and boundaries changed.
3. **Personal action**: identify the user's supported design, implementation, testing, analysis, or operational work.
4. **Technical difficulty**: identify observable complexity, constraints, edge cases, and trade-offs.
5. **Quality and correctness**: identify tests, validation, safety, compatibility, and review evidence.
6. **System effect**: identify supported capability, reliability, performance, maintainability, or operability changes.
7. **User or business effect**: identify only independently supported outcomes and metrics.
8. **Collaboration and attribution**: identify authors, co-authors, reviewers, dependencies, and boundaries.
9. **Learning and growth**: separate demonstrated skill from user-confirmed learning or responsibility change.
10. **Reusability and leverage**: identify supported reuse, automation, standards, or reduced repeated work.

Treat deletions, tests, configuration, stability work, incident fixes, migrations, documentation tied to behavior, and simplification as possible high-value work. Judge them by supported risk or capability changes, not line count.

## Major-Output Synthesis

For each major output, identify the supported scope, code path, personal contribution, validation, and user or business value. Separate authored evidence from collaborator context, and never turn a team result, adjacent capability, or inferred intent into a personal claim. Use the target role to prioritize the most relevant technical decisions and trade-offs. Carry unresolved evidence forward as explicit unknowns or questions for the final document.
